I recently read cricketer R Ashwin's book [I Have the Streets, a Kutti Cricket Story](https://www.amazon.in/Have-Streets-Kutti-Cricket-Story/dp/0670094749). I found it to be a very enjoyable read - with a vivid description of 90s Chennai, PSBB schools, cricket, and how an entire family, a middle class household in Chennai, innovated, experimented and created an ecosystem and ultimately set the stage for Ashwin to succeed as a cricketer. The book has vivid references to [Mankading](https://sportstar.thehindu.com/cricket/ipl/faqs/what-is-mankading-non-striker-run-out-mcc-rules-laws-ipl-2023-spirit-of-the-game/article66628699.ece), how we met his wife Prithi, [the carrom ball](https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-news/112288/how-ashwin-developed-his-carrom-ball) and so on. 

![[ashwin-book.jpg|center|225x300]]

The book starts off with Ashwin's childhood, the health issues he had to face as a kid and how his entire family - his Amma (mother), Appa (father) and Thatha (granddad) passionately helped their kid succeed in cricket. Ashwin's dad being a cricketer himself, who had to forego his cricketing career for a job at the Indian railways was passionate to help his son succeed. Ashwin shared that love and passion for cricket tremendously as well. His vivid accounts of his cricketing analyses later in the book, tales of how their family tried to setup a pitch at their backyard (using cow dung !), and how each family member meticulously executed their role to perfection is very endearing. 

Ashwin's descriptions of 80s Chennai, the street side soup stalls, bowling in cricket alleys in West Mambalam, restricted rules to prevent the ball from breaking windows or going over terraces, under arm cricket were very nostalgic. 

In the later part of the book, Ashwin also writes about playing under MS Dhoni, his captaincy style and how MSD saw Ashwin as a gutsy player who at some point was his lieutenant to bowl in tough situations (like the final over in 2013 ICC Champions Trophy). His equation with VB Chandrashekhar (who played a pivotal role in developing CSK into the beast that it is today), former TN coach WV Raman, is also explored. 

Overall, I found the very book to be very engaging. Ashwin and his co-author Siddharth Monga have made an excellent account of the sacrifices a family makes to make a kid successful in one of the most competitive sports in India, in addition to a vivid description of 80s and 90s Chennai and several interesting incidents from the Indian cricket team and Chennai Super Kings.
